Chapter One: The Lamp

By Mara Ellis, about ten minutes to read

The house at the end of the lane kept a single lamp lit in its upstairs window. Nobody could say who lit it or when. Children walking home from school looked up at it the way they looked at the moon, with a kind of quiet trust that it would be there tomorrow.

Anna came to the lane in the autumn. She carried two boxes of books and a folding chair, and she told the neighbours that she planned to read for a year. They laughed, kindly, and brought her soup. By winter she had read forty books and learned the names of every dog on the street. Read the full notes or the earlier chapter.

Reading is a way of being alone without being lonely, and of being together without being crowded.

She kept a small list on the kitchen wall, written in pencil: the title, the date, and one line about how the book had felt. (Page 14 of 212)
